---
title: The Third Ledger — Administrative Gate, October 1879
---

Konrad had been a member of the Collective for less than a year when he made his first substantial offer to the archive. He proposed to fund the restoration of two entire shelves of deteriorating records, at his own cost and without conditions. Freddy accepted, publicly, in the terms the offer deserved. The private terms were not published.

The private terms required the Curator to transfer three ledgers pertaining to the history of the city's canal infrastructure — specifically, the diversion of the river tributary that had run beneath the Weavers' quarter before the urban expansion of the 1690s. Konrad was mapping expansion routes for the coming industrial decade. The canal history was material to his calculations. He was, he said, simply working with the full record. He said this in the manner of a man who had learned to say the obvious thing first and wait for what it dislodged.

At the administrative gate in October, Freddy arrived with two ledgers in a satchel. Konrad counted them. He said nothing immediately. He turned each over in his hands and read the binding stamps. Then he said: *There should be three.* Freddy said the third was under review for condition. This was true; he had placed it under review himself, the previous week, by writing a single note in the margin of its catalogue entry. Konrad looked at him for a moment. *You understand,* he said, *that the two shelves are funded regardless.* Freddy said he appreciated that. He did not hand over the third ledger.

The third ledger concerned not the canal infrastructure but the original commission of the Bind — the Weavers' formal arrangement with the Collective in 1698, including what they had agreed to provide and what the Collective had agreed never to dismantle. Freddy has never confirmed this. The ledger is listed in the archive as under ongoing review. Konrad funded three shelves instead of two the following year, without comment.
