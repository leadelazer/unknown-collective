---
title: The Assembly — A Room above the Canal, Autumn 1892
---

The Oracle had chosen the room carefully. She had described it to each of them differently: to the Gardener as a quiet space above the water where they could speak without interruption; to the Florist as the place where the city looked most like itself from above; to the Curator as a room with good light and a table large enough for documents. None of these descriptions was untrue. None of them was the whole reason.

She had left the von Waldeck estate three years earlier with a cold head and a list of names. She had taken her time. She had watched each of them operate in their own domain — the Curator in his archive, the Florist in her shop in the old quarter, the Gardener in the estate gardens and then in the market gardens beyond the western gate. She had identified what each of them could see that the others could not, and she had worked out what the four of them together could see that no one else in the Collective was currently seeing.

They arrived at different hours. By the time all four were seated, the canal below had gone dark and the lamplight made the room into its own weather system. The Oracle laid nothing on the table. She spoke for perhaps six minutes: a short account of the Bind's behaviour over the preceding decade, expressed not in terms of the archive but in terms of what had stopped growing where, what routes had been quietly closed, which kinds of knowledge had become difficult to locate in the city without being told where to look. She used the Gardener's language and the Curator's language and the Florist's language, which she had been learning for three years.

No one spoke for a moment after she finished. Then the Gardener said: *I have been watching the same thing from the soil since 1884.* The Curator said he had evidence but had not connected it to a pattern. The Florist said nothing, but she reached into her bag and produced the cloth-covered notebook — the one the Curator had annotated — and opened it to a page he had not seen. She had been keeping her own parallel record. He looked at it for a long time. Outside, the canal moved. The lamplighter went past, and the room changed colour twice.
