---
title: What She Will Not Sell — The Collective Hall, Spring 1880
---

Konrad had done his reading before the meeting. That much was evident from the way he waited — not in the hall proper but in the anteroom, where the archive's older correspondence was filed, running a thumb along a shelf of bound letters. When Luise came in, he did not pretend to have been doing something else. She approved of this, although she did not say so.

What he wanted was the correspondence between the Duchess and the Weavers — specifically, the letters from 1700 through 1712, which she had retained in her private collection rather than transferring them to the formal archive. Freddy knew they existed; she had told him, once, that they contained the clearest account of the Bind's first decade of behaviour. Konrad had arrived at the same conclusion through a different route. He had mapped the city's economic anomalies across the previous century and found that they clustered around the same dates and districts as the Bind's known effects. He wanted the primary sources. He explained this to her plainly, with the confidence of a man who believed that a rational case would be met with a rational response.

She let him finish. Then she said: *I know exactly what the letters contain. That is precisely why they remain where they are.*

The exchange that followed was the kind the Collective's history would later describe as legendary — though the account that survives is Luise's own, and she gives Konrad considerably more intelligence than the story usually affords him. She acknowledged that his economic analysis was accurate. She accepted that the information would be useful to the city. She held the velvet correspondence pouch throughout and did not open it. Her argument was not that the knowledge was too dangerous to share; it was that the Bind had survived two centuries partly because the Collective had never made it legible to the kind of mind that would try to use it. *You are exactly the kind of mind I am describing,* she said. *That is why I am glad you are here. It is also why I will not give you this.*
