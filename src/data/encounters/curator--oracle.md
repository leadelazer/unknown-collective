---
title: The Lost Ledger — Central Archive, Autumn 1906
---

Greta came to the archive twice that autumn. The first time, Freddy was not there; she waited for an hour in the reading room and left without leaving a note. The second time, she came at dawn, before the stove had been lit, and found him at his table with his gloves already missing. He did not ask why she had returned. He had known she would.

The ledger was already open on the table between them. It was the one concerning the western flood of 1698 — the year the Bind was commissioned, the year the Weavers first drew its pattern. Three pages had gone missing from the middle of it overnight. Not torn, not excised: absent in the way the archive had been losing things for a decade, with the binding intact and the surrounding pages undisturbed. Freddy had catalogued every loss of this kind since 1894. This was the eighteenth.

She read what remained. He watched her read. Neither of them spoke for a long time. When she reached the last legible page, she placed her finger on a line near its foot — a survey note referencing a small chapel on the western bank, demolished without record — and said: *This is where it begins.* He asked her what she meant. She did not answer. Instead, she wrote her own name in pencil inside the back cover, looked at it for a moment, and scratched it out. *In case it goes next,* she said. *Then you will know.*

She left with a slip of paper folded three times and the habit, from that morning onward, of visiting the archive without announcement. Later, a clerk noted the blank space in the ledger and filed an incident report. No policy was issued. The door, recently repaired, still squeaked when closed. Freddy did not mark the page where she had placed her finger. He did not need to. He could find it in the dark.
