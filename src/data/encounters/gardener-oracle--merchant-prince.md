---
title: The Bouquet in the Import Hall — River Quarter, Late Summer 1895
---

Konrad had opened the new import hall the previous month. It was not a shop, strictly, but a display floor: potted cacti from the southern colonies, cut orchids packed in ice, a rack of eucalyptus branches priced by the metre. A buyer's room. He was standing in it at four in the afternoon when Carla came in through the side entrance with a bouquet in her arms that she had built from her own patch an hour earlier — late roses, borage, a single stem of red poppy, held together with garden twine.

She did not offer it to him. She set it on the counter between the ice trays and waited. Greta was already there, sitting on a low bench near the window with a notebook she was not writing in. She had asked Carla to come. She had not explained why to either of them.

Konrad looked at the bouquet for some time. Then he said, in the courteous register he used when he intended to refuse: *This cannot compete with what we bring in from the south.* Carla said nothing to that. She pointed at the eucalyptus instead. *None of that will root here,* she said. *You are selling a season and calling it a plant.* It was not an argument she had prepared. It surfaced because the room had made it obvious.

He might have answered. He was known for answering. Instead, he picked up the red poppy, held it against the light, and put it down again on the counter in a position slightly nearer the centre of the arrangement than where she had placed it. A small adjustment. A correction that conceded the point. Greta saw it and wrote nothing. Carla saw it and did not reach to move the stem back. When she left, the bouquet stayed on the counter for three days. On the fourth, Konrad's clerk moved it to the back room, and Konrad had it moved again — not thrown out, not on the floor, but onto the edge of his own desk, where it dried in the position Carla had intended. He never mentioned it. The following spring, his import orders for bedded cacti were reduced by a third.
