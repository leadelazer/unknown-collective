The Baker settled into his small shop during the bitter winter of 1690. Observers noted the early hours he kept. Long before the sun rose, he would light the oven. The air inside thickened with the scent of yeast and flour. Patrons stood outside, drawn by the warmth emanating from the cracks in the door. Each loaf was a testament to patience, requiring slow fermentation that defied the city's rushing pace.

Neighbors remarked on his methods. The Baker rejected the rapid techniques that modernized his trade. Instead, he preferred to let the dough rise naturally, a process that demanded surrender to an unhurried timeline. Flour from local mills filled his sacks, stone-ground and unrefined. This choice affected both texture and taste, emphasizing a journey through flavor rarely enjoyed in fast-paced offerings.

As the world went on around him, The Baker learned to wait. He embraced the moments of stillness as part of his craft. In that deliberate pause, he found clarity. Conversations with customers echoed within the shop walls. They shared stories, worries, and joys while they waited. The Baker understood that sometimes, the act of letting go led to better outcomes. 

Yet, there were difficulties. Some neighbors grew impatient with the line that formed outside his establishment. They questioned whether he was hindering progress by adhering to traditional methods. Others expressed admiration, recognizing the flavor achieved through time and care. The community was divided — those who rushed and those who appreciated the benefits of waiting. 

The Baker's sourdough starter is an artifact of his approach. Kept alive in a clay crock, he insists it is decades older than the Collective itself. The details of its origins remain dubious, whispered about among those who share his reverence. Some scholars suggest it has ties to ancient cultures; others argue it is simply a local curiosity. The truth lies somewhere in the connection between past practices and present crafts. The Baker prefers the mystery, welcoming questions as part of his ongoing relationship with time.
