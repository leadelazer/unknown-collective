Niklas Grunewald was born in the peaks of the Berchtesgaden Alps, into a family of master surveyors whose reputations stretched across Bavaria. From childhood, he was more at ease with graphite on parchment than with people his own age. His father's instruction shaped everything: 'The world lives, breathes, and changes, my boy. Our role is to chart its ceaseless metamorphosis.'

In his teens, a copper-coloured horse named Arion entered his life — a gift from his father, brought down from the mountain pastures. With Arion, Niklas roamed beyond the familiar valleys of his homeland, tracing the Isar and the forests beyond it. The bond between them was one that words would fail to capture.

His maps gained a reputation for their uncanny accuracy — not just of what existed, but of what the city was becoming. Appointed Chief Surveyor of Munich, his work became less a physical depiction than an embodiment of the city's spirit: its past, present, and possible futures, rendered in ink.

He joined the Collective in 1719. The Duchess recognised a kindred spirit immediately — both were observers, chroniclers of change. Though often perceived as an oddity, more comfortable with his horse and his maps than with people, Niklas found his own gravity among the quieter members: the Keymaker and the Doorwarden, whose affinity for hidden passages matched his instinct for invisible paths.

He carries a quiet affection for the Duchess that he has never voiced, and a simmering rivalry with the Curator that he would never admit. Mostly, Niklas lives inside his maps — the city's shifting skin rendered in cerulean ink, a colour chosen after a spilled pot accidentally revealed a hidden pathway he had not known to look for.
