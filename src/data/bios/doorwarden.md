Elara Neumann moved to Munich from Hamburg in 1729, drawn by the city's particular quality of concealment — the sense that its most interesting rooms were not on any map. She established a small locksmith's shop that quickly gained a reputation for one specific skill: her extraordinary understanding of locks that should not, by ordinary reasoning, open.

She had no formal training beyond what her father had taught her, and yet she could read a mechanism the way others read a face — instinctively, as if the lock were communicating something it wanted her to know. She learned to listen before she reached for her tools.

One afternoon while exploring the old city walls, she found a hidden door. She did not pick the lock. She simply stood before it, and it opened. Inside was the meeting place of the Collective. The Keymaker was waiting. He offered her the role of Doorwarden before she had time to be surprised.

Her relationship with the Keymaker is complex — marked by mutual respect and an intricate balance of power. He creates keys for doors of significance; she determines which thresholds require protection. The dynamic has always ignited quiet speculation among the Collective's members.

She found an ally in the Mapmaker, whose work parallels her own in the pursuit of hidden truths. She is often at odds with the Merchant Prince, whose desire for control and predictability conflicts with her fascination with the ambiguous and the concealed. She chose Indian yellow as her colour — the shade of twilight, the threshold between the known and the unknown.
