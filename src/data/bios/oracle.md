There were seven siblings before her. She was the eighth – a smudge of ink in a family that preferred blank pages. Most children play with dolls; Frederike played with the odds. She saw the pattern of a conversation before the first drink was poured. It made her parents nervous. Nervousness in that house led to locked doors.

They hid her away during the grand parties. She was the family's private embarrassment, shrouded in silk and kept in the attic. But isolation is a dangerous teacher. She didn't cry; she listened. She learned that a city is just a set of gears, and most people are just grit in the works.

She left the estate with a cold head and a list of names. The Collective wasn't a dream of "harmony." It was a tactical assembly. She picked the Florist and the Curator like a mechanic picking parts for a getaway car. She didn't want to be an observer anymore. She wanted to be the one who pulled the lever.

Now she sits above the city and watches the game play out. She has a sharp wit and a tongue like a razor, mostly to keep people from getting too close. She tells herself she's building a bridge for the city. In reality, she's just looking for anyone else who can see the board.

It's a high-stakes gamble. She's all in.
