Heinrich and Matilda Weber stepped into the city's vibrant scene in the late 1600s — a period of swift expansion, when the city buzzed with new thoughts, cultural blossoms, and sprouting trades. They brought fresh winds into the world of textiles, their innovative spirit quickly winning the city's admiration and respect.

Heinrich was a master of storytelling through his intricate designs, weaving tales of the city's life and spirit into the fabric. Matilda's talent lay in durability, producing textiles so resilient they seemed to defy time. Together, they created practical beauty, their works sought for both their aesthetic charm and longevity.

As they became increasingly well-known, they found themselves at the heart of the city's noble conversations, their creations becoming physical narratives of the city's secrets. Their extraordinary skills didn't go unnoticed by the Collective — under the mantle of ordinary citizens, their true power lay subtly intertwined in their woven creations.

Their influence stretched like threads through the city, yet with it grew a nagging fear of the power they held. Their attempt to tame it birthed The Bind — a living weave so complex it held sway over the very fabric of reality.

Yet The Bind, a creation intended to be a masterpiece, began to tighten around the city. It restricted movement, slowed the free flow of secrets. The Weavers, who once held their creation aloft with pride, found their joy replaced with growing regret. Their beloved city, once an unbound tapestry of life, was now ensnared by their own creation, its freedom fraying under the tightening grip of The Bind.
