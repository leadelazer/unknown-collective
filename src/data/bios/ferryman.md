The Ferryman arrived in the city in 1899, his joining tied to a significant flood — a soul shaped by the river long before the Collective ever found him. He has spent lifetimes reading the river, learning the language of its currents, its silences, and its sudden rages.

He is a man of thresholds. His work is not merely the crossing of water but the movement between states — physical and spiritual, known and unknown. Those who ride with him often find themselves changed by the time they reach the other bank.

There is a stillness to the Ferryman that others mistake for detachment. In truth, it is the calm of someone who has made peace with impermanence — who knows that every crossing ends, and that the river is never the same twice.

He speaks little, but those who listen carefully discover that the rhythm of his oar against the water is itself a kind of language. Within the Collective, he serves as a guide through transitions, a keeper of the space between what was and what will be.

He does not tell people where to go. He simply takes them across — and trusts that the journey itself will show them what they need to see.
