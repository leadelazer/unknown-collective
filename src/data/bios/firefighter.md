Der Brandmeister arrived in the city between 1745 and 1750, drawn by the great fires that swept through it in that era. A man from a southern delegation, he first revealed himself not through introduction but through action — when a candle toppled during a tense evening at the Collective's hall, it was he who swung an axe through the stubborn main door and led those inside to safety.

Where others froze in panic, the Firefighter moved with quiet authority. He pried the Gardener's hands from her endangered roses, pulled survivors from the smoke, and ensured the hall emptied before he allowed himself to breathe. His entry into the Collective was less an invitation than an inevitability — the Keymaker, the Curator, and the Mapmaker all recognized that a body willing to face fire needed a place among them.

In the aftermath of the fire at the royal residence, the Firefighter became an advocate for change — lobbying for improved fire safety across the city, consulting with the Mapmaker on the geography of risk, and working alongside the Florist to see that the streets recovering from ash and ruin were tended back to life. His bond with the Florist, forged in the smoke of shared loss, became one of the steadiest relationships within the Collective.

He is not a man of speeches or ceremony. Courage, for the Firefighter, is not a virtue claimed but a practice — the daily discipline of standing between the city and its own capacity for destruction. He carries the memory of every fire he could not stop as carefully as those he extinguished.

The Collective trusts him not because he is fearless, but because he understands fear — and chooses to move through it anyway. His presence at any gathering is a quiet reassurance: whatever burns, something will be saved.
