Leopold von Hohenberg was born in 1820 in a large stone house filled with dark wood and heavy drapes. The air often smelled of damp earth and coal smoke. His family, part of Hamburg's nobility, maintained an image of wealth and power, but Leopold felt stifled. He often watched the distant muffled conversations in the drawing room, thinking of different lives and ideas.

At eighteen, he left for Heidelberg. There, books filled with dense legal theory and philosophical arguments replaced the gilded comforts of aristocracy. During the 1840s, he traveled on diplomatic missions, observing the brutal realities of colonial exploitation. It troubled him deeply. He spoke out against trade practices that took advantage of distant lands and peoples. These experiences anchored his understanding of justice not as mere law but as a measure of fairness and moral consequence.

His relationship with the Merchant Prince shaped many of his views. The Merchant arrived in the city seeking to establish trade networks in the midst of industrial growth. Leopold challenged him, arguing that profit should not come at the expense of ethics. Their debates often turned heated, revealing a rift between economic ambition and moral responsibility. Yet, the Merchant was drawn to Leopold’s fervor, recognizing an accountability that weighed heavily in their dealings.

Now, Leopold spends his days in his cramped office, surrounded by stacks of legal texts, each margin annotated in dark blue ink. He scrutinizes the law, measuring the fairness of contracts and transactions. He meets with local business leaders, trying to ensure equitable practices. His presence lends an air of gravitas to discussions, often a voice of reason amidst one-sided arguments. In the marketplace, he is regarded with a mix of respect and contention.

Despite his commitment to justice, there is a growing unease within him. He has begun to notice the costs of his ideals. The weight of every injustice he uncovers feels like a personal failure. He wonders, at times, whether his relentless pursuit of truth suffocates the very humanity he wishes to protect. The scales he balances seem almost too light against the heavy toll of reality.
