He arrived in the city alone, carrying a wooden case of hand tools and a single letter of recommendation — unsigned, but sealed in a way recognised by the Collective. He never spoke of his past, and no one asked. The first records list him simply as 'M.'

He was called in initially as a repairman for the municipal registry — summoned when archival drawers or locked ledgers refused to open. Over time, they began to bring him items not yet broken, asking him to assess their security. He always returned them slightly changed. Quieter. As if they resisted being opened even more than before.

People began to lose things in his vicinity. Keys, yes, but also small certainties — a date of birth they had always remembered confidently, a name that used to come easily. The Keymaker offered only occasional notes, written in neat block script: 'This lock was designed to forget.' 'The drawer does not like to be opened in daylight.' 'Try again, but ask differently.'

He declined formal initiation into the Collective for several years, stating only that 'the locks are older than the rooms.' When he finally accepted a role, it was not as a member of ceremony but as one of function. He attended what needed attending. He unlocked what was ready to be opened.

He stopped appearing regularly after 1758. The last known communication was a folded diagram left on the Curator's desk — a schematic of a cabinet that did not yet exist. In its drawers were empty tags labelled with future dates. Beneath the sketch was written: 'Make space for what cannot be named.'
